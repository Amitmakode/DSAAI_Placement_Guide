# Person ReID Tracker — Interview Preparation

## 🎤 Storytelling: How to Introduce This Project

Use this as your 60–90 second answer when they ask "Tell me about a project you've worked on."

> "I built a **Person Re-Identification and Presence Tracking system**. The idea was simple: if a camera sees a person walk in and out of frame, or move between multiple cameras, the system should still recognize *who* they are — not just detect 'a person', but say 'this is Shubham' — and track how long they were present.
>
> To do this, I used a **3-stage pipeline**. First, **YOLO11** detects people in each video frame and gives me a segmentation mask, so I can cut out just the person and remove background noise. Second, I extract two types of identity features: a **body appearance embedding** using OSNet (which looks at clothing, shape, posture) and a **face embedding** using InsightFace. I combine both into a **hybrid similarity score**, so even if the face isn't visible, the body embedding alone can still re-identify the person, and vice versa.
>
> Third, I built a **custom multi-object tracker** using a Kalman Filter for motion prediction and the Hungarian algorithm for matching detections to existing tracks frame-by-frame. Once a track is confirmed, it's matched against a **gallery of enrolled people** — each enrolled person has an averaged embedding built from ~10 reference images.
>
> Finally, all this feeds into a **presence engine** that logs IN/OUT events and total time spent, storing everything in a **MySQL database** with soft-delete/audit trails, and I built a **Streamlit dashboard** for admins to enroll new people, view analytics, and archive old runs.
>
> The most interesting challenge was avoiding false matches — I handled that with a **similarity margin check**, so if the top-2 candidate scores are too close, the system says 'Unknown' instead of guessing wrong."

**Tip:** Practice this out loud 3–4 times. Interviewers love when you can explain flow → challenge → solution without reading notes.

## 📋 20 Easy Scenario-Based Q&A

**1. Q: In simple words, what does your project do?**
A: It watches a video feed, detects people, and if that person is already "enrolled" (registered) in the system, it recognizes their name and tracks how long they stayed — like a smart attendance system using a camera instead of ID cards.

**2. Q: Why did you need both a "detector" and a "tracker"? Aren't they the same?**
A: No. The detector (YOLO) finds people in a *single* frame — it doesn't know if it's the same person as the previous frame. The tracker connects those detections *across* frames, giving each person a consistent ID as they move, even for a few frames where detection might fail.

**3. Q: Suppose a person turns their back to the camera. Will your system still recognize them?**
A: Yes, partially. Since I use a **hybrid score** (body + face), if the face isn't visible, the system relies only on the body embedding (clothing, shape). It won't be as confident, but it can still match if the body embedding score crosses the threshold.

**4. Q: What happens if two enrolled people look very similar and the system isn't sure who is who?**
A: I added a **margin check** — it compares the best match score with the second-best. If they're too close (within a small margin), the system labels the person "Unknown" instead of risking a wrong guess. Better to say "I don't know" than to misidentify someone.

**5. Q: A person walks behind a pillar for 2 seconds and reappears. Does your system think they left and a new person entered?**
A: No — I use an **absence timeout** in the presence tracker. A short disappearance (a few frames) doesn't immediately trigger an "OUT" event. Only if the person is missing beyond the timeout does the system count them as having left.

**6. Q: How do you actually "recognize" a new person — walk me through enrollment.**
A: An admin runs the enrollment script, which opens a webcam. It captures ~10 clear images of the person's body (and face, if visible) with quality checks like blur detection and minimum size. Then a separate script averages all those images into one embedding vector per person and saves it — that's their "reference" in the gallery.

**7. Q: Why average multiple images into one embedding instead of storing all 10 separately?**
A: A single photo might have odd lighting, angle, or pose. Averaging multiple embeddings gives a more stable, general representation of the person and reduces the influence of any one bad image — and it's much faster to compare 1 vector instead of 10 during real-time matching.

**8. Q: What's the role of the Kalman Filter here?**
A: It predicts where a tracked person's bounding box will be in the *next* frame, based on their previous position and speed. This helps the tracker keep following a person smoothly even if the detector misses them for a frame or two.

**9. Q: Why did you use the Hungarian Algorithm?**
A: When you have multiple tracked people and multiple new detections in a frame, you need to match them optimally (this old track = this new detection). The Hungarian algorithm finds the best overall pairing (lowest total cost) instead of just greedily matching the first ones it sees, which avoids ID-swapping mistakes.

**10. Q: Suppose the camera detects 5 people, but only 3 are enrolled. What happens to the other 2?**
A: They still get tracked (motion + a track ID), but since their embeddings don't match anyone in the gallery above the threshold, they're labeled "Unknown". They aren't counted in the presence/attendance stats, which only track *known* identities.

**11. Q: What's stored in your MySQL database?**
A: Three main things per "run" (a camera session): (1) the run info — source, start/end time, (2) per-person presence summary — total time, entry/exit counts, (3) individual IN/OUT events with timestamps. There's also soft-delete columns and archive tables for audit history.

**12. Q: Why "soft delete" instead of just deleting a row?**
A: If an admin accidentally archives a run, we don't want that data gone forever. Soft delete just marks a `deleted_at` timestamp, and a trigger copies the row into an archive table. So nothing is truly lost, and it's reversible/auditable.

**13. Q: How does the Streamlit dashboard fit into this project?**
A: It's the admin-facing UI. Three pages: Home (overview stats), Analytics (pick a past run, see who was detected, IN/OUT timeline), and Admin (password-protected — enroll new people by uploading images, archive old runs).

**14. Q: If someone uploads new photos for an already-enrolled person, does the system automatically use them?**
A: Not instantly — uploading deletes the old cached embedding file (`embedding_mean.npy`). The next time the pipeline runs, it notices there's no cached embedding and recalculates it from all images in that person's folder, including the new ones.

**15. Q: What would happen if you removed the face embedding entirely and used only body appearance?**
A: The system would still work, but be less accurate — body/clothing can be similar between people, and if someone changes clothes, it may fail to match them. Face adds a stronger, more unique signal when visible.

**16. Q: What if two people are wearing identical clothes?**
A: The body embedding alone might confuse them. This is exactly why I combined body + face — if the face is visible, that similarity dominates and separates the two correctly.

**17. Q: How do you make sure the pipeline doesn't crash if the database is down?**
A: The MySQL store is optional — if `mysql_host` isn't set, the pipeline just skips saving. Also, I wrapped save operations in try/except with proper logging, so a database failure doesn't crash video processing, but is reported clearly at the end.

**18. Q: Why do you compute time using real elapsed seconds rather than frame count?**
A: Different videos/cameras run at different FPS. If I counted frames, a slow camera (15 FPS) vs a fast one (30 FPS) would give wrong duration estimates. Using actual timestamps makes the "time present" accurate regardless of frame rate.

**19. Q: Is this project doing face recognition or person re-identification — what's the difference?**
A: Face recognition only works when a clear face is visible. Person re-identification (ReID) is broader — it tries to recognize a person from their *overall appearance* (clothing, body shape) even without a face. My project does both together for stronger accuracy.

**20. Q: If you had more time, what would you improve?**
A: A few things — support multi-camera handoff (recognizing the same person across different camera feeds), moving to cloud/object storage for videos instead of storing them in MySQL as BLOBs, and possibly re-training or fine-tuning the ReID model on domain-specific data (e.g., uniforms) for better accuracy.
